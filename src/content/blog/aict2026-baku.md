Some papers take more than research. This one also needed a plan for flights, a
registration fee, a hotel and a lot of patience. Here is the story of how our
paper made it into the programme of **AICT2026**, an international conference on
information and communication technologies held at ADA University in Baku on
14–16 October 2026.

## The paper

Our paper is called **"Explainable Machine Learning Framework for Multi-Stage
Arterial Hypertension Prediction"**. It builds on earlier work in the lab on
predicting arterial hypertension from clinical data. This time we wanted to go
further than a single yes-or-no answer: the framework predicts the *stage* of
hypertension, and it explains which factors drive each prediction.

The explanations matter as much as the accuracy. A model that says "stage 2"
without saying why is hard to trust in a medical setting. Making the model's
reasoning visible was the part of the project we cared about most.

The work started like most lab projects do: a dataset, many evenings of
cleaning and experiments, and long discussions about which results we could
honestly stand behind. Then came the writing, the rewriting and the
submission. A few weeks later the email arrived: **accepted**.

## A Tuesday of good news

An accepted paper is only half the job. Someone still has to present it.
Travelling to Baku as students is not cheap, so we went to the university to
ask for support.

With the help of **Bakhtiyor Meraliyev**
([bakhtiyor.meraliyev@sdu.edu.kz](mailto:bakhtiyor.meraliyev@sdu.edu.kz)) and
**Ualikhan Sadyk**
([ualikhan.sadyk@sdu.edu.kz](mailto:ualikhan.sadyk@sdu.edu.kz)), we got a "yes"
on a Tuesday: SDU agreed to support our trip to Baku. For a student team it felt
like a second acceptance letter.

## When the plan met reality

Then the logistics started. Plane tickets, the conference registration fee and
the hotel all had to come together at the same time, and they did not. Each
problem was solvable on its own. Together, with the conference only days away,
they turned into a puzzle we could not finish in time.

For a moment it looked as if all that work might end with an accepted paper
and no presentation.

## Plan B: presenting online

We did not want to give up on the talk, so we contacted the organisers and
asked whether we could defend the paper online. They agreed.

Our paper is now in the official
[conference programme](https://www.aict.info/2026/info/AICT2026-Conference-Program.pdf)
in **Online Session II: ICT in Medicine and Health Care**. We will present live
on Microsoft Teams on **Thursday, 15 October 2026, 15:45–16:00 Baku time**
(16:45 in Astana), followed by questions from the session.

## What we learned

- **Ask for help early.** Support from our supervisors and the university made
  the trip possible on paper, and their advice kept us going when it fell apart.
- **Start the logistics the day the paper is accepted.** Visas, tickets, fees
  and hotels take longer than any revision.
- **An online talk is still a real talk.** The paper, the questions and the
  discussion are the same. What matters is that the work gets presented.
- **Keep going when the first plan breaks.** We ended up in the programme
  because we kept looking for another way.

Thank you to Bakhtiyor Meraliyev, Ualikhan Sadyk and SDU University for believing in
this work, and to the AICT2026 organisers for making the online session
possible. Wish us luck on Thursday. We will share how the talk went.
